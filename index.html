Chapter 3: Financial Environment

Types of Companies in Indonesia

Comprehensive breakdown based strictly on Chapter 3 module.

State-Owned Company (BUMN): A business entity whose capital is wholly or partly owned by the Indonesian government. There are two primary forms of BUMN: Perum and Persero.

a. Public Company (Perum)

BUMN

Oriented towards serving public interest while seeking profits. Capital is state-owned derived from loans. Led by directors, holds state facilities, and civil servant employees.

Key Characteristics

  • Ultimate goal: Serve public interest + seek profit
  • State-owned capital with proceeds derived from loans
  • Led by directors; Has state facility
  • Employees are state enterprise employees
  • Moves on vital business; Has socioeconomic function
  • Legal entity: can prosecute/be prosecuted under civil law
Example:

Perum DAMRI (Public Transport Services)

b. Liability Company (Persero)

BUMN

Capital is in the form of stock (shares) partly or wholly owned by state assets. Main purpose is gain profit and provide public services. Shares are traded on stock exchanges.

Key Characteristics

  • Main objective is commercial profit
  • Capital derived partly/entirely from state wealth (stocks)
  • Led by professional directors
  • Employee status is private employee
  • Business entity written as PT (Persero)
Examples:

PT Garuda Indonesia (Persero) Tbk, PT Angkasa Pura (Persero)

Private Owned Company (BUMS): Established and financed by a person or group of private individuals. Consists of Firma (Fa), Commanditaire Vennootschap (CV), Limited Company (PT), and Sole Proprietorship (UD).

1. Firma (Fa)

Business entity established by 2 or more persons under a shared name. Executed via agreement deed before a notary.

Benefits: • Easy to establish
• Greater financial capability
• Joint decision making
• Clear legal status
• Division of labor by expertise
Disadvantages: • Unlimited debt liability for members
• Less reliable continuity (disbands if member leaves)
• Internal conflicts threaten survival

2. Commanditaire Vennootschap (CV)

Established by 2+ partners consisting of Active Partners (manage & run firm with full liability) and Silent Partners (provide capital only, limited liability).

Benefits: • Easy to establish
• Amass large capital
• More credit & expansion opportunity
• Verified management
Disadvantages: • Unlimited liability for general/active partners
• Less reliable survival
• Hard to pull back investment

3. Limited Company (PT)

Capital is divided into shares/stocks. Liability is strictly limited to capital invested. Includes Closed PT (family/private group) and Open PT (stocks sold to public/going public).

Benefits: • Limited debt liability
• Company survival guaranteed
• Shares traded easily
• Easy to attract public capital
Disadvantages: • High establishment cost
• Tax reports required
• Special permit needed to open

4. Sole Proprietorship (UD)

Simplest business form requiring only 1 person. No legal difference between owner and business entity.

5 Advantages: 1. Less paperwork
2. Easier tax process
3. Fewer registration fees
4. Straightforward banking
5. Simplified ownership
3 Disadvantages: 1. No liability protection
2. Harder to get financing & business credit
3. Harder to sell business
